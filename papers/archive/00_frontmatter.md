## Abstract

Gene-family studies find their genes through protein databases, which hold
only what genome annotation delivers. We measured how completely the public
record holds the inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor genes (*ITPR1*,
*ITPR2*, *ITPR3*) that 309 vertebrate genome assemblies carry. The ground
truth was a protein-to-genome sweep that owes nothing to annotation. The
ryanodine receptors, a size-matched sister family sharing every diagnostic
domain, were scored alongside as a control. Of 923 genome-by-paralogue
genes demonstrated in the DNA, 744 (80.6 %) cannot be reached by any
protein-database search, against 196 of 309 ryanodine receptor cells. Of
932 scorable loci in assemblies that ship a gene set, 689 are delivered as
one complete model, which is no worse than the control's 734 of 942 (q =
0.13). The archive decides the outcome: 1,161 of 1,175 loci in RefSeq
annotations are complete against 262 of 699 in submitter GenBank
annotations. The gap narrows but does not close on assemblies able to carry
the gene. Protein records name the family and paralogue correctly almost
everywhere, but of 11,402 full-length records 3,872 carry a placeholder
gene symbol and 2,395 carry none. All 15 vertebrate reference proteomes with no family protein are
gene-caller failures. Two failures validated to the exon are intact,
canonically spliced genes, and reads from 67 public RNA-seq runs cross 298
of the 314 junctions no annotated model spans. We release 297
coordinate-level corrections with their evidence.
