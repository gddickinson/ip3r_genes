## Discussion

The IP₃-binding core is not the most constrained part of the IP₃ receptor.
Paired per orthologue across roughly 250 sequences per paralogue, the pore
module leads in *ITPR1* and *ITPR3* and the two are level in *ITPR2*. The
module that gives the family its name and its diagnostic signature is the less
conserved of the two. Naming a family after its diagnostic domain is a
statement about what that domain identifies, and it does not predict what
selection holds most tightly.

The answer depends on one boundary. With the luminal loop counted as pore,
all three paralogues reverse and the core leads by about five identity
points. The loop is solvent-facing, poorly resolved in the cryo-EM maps
[R24, R59] and the least conserved sequence in the receptor, and it lies
inside the same database domain as the gate. Any comparison of ligand-binding
and pore regions in this family, and plausibly in its sister family, has to
declare that boundary to be interpretable.

At the ligand site itself, selection holds a pocket and not a contact set.
The ten residues that touch IP₃ are special against the rest of the
binding core and not against the other residues within 15 Å of the ligand,
and constraint shows no step at the contact radius. The same shape appears on
an independent axis, the per-site substitution rate on a vertebrate codon
tree. The structural studies that define the binding core describe a pocket
lined by basic residues from two domains [R05, R57]; our result says that
evolution treats that whole pocket, rather than its phosphate-coordinating
residues alone, as the conserved unit.

Losing the enzyme that makes IP₃ does not relax the core. Sixty-four
eukaryotic proteomes carry the receptor and no phosphoinositide-specific
phospholipase C, most of them oomycetes and early-diverging fungi. The
comparison that looks positive when pooled is a comparison of distant against
near, and it vanishes when each record is matched on divergence. The null is
bounded by the ryanodine receptors, which have the same pore and no IP₃ site
and move the paired difference roughly ten times further than the matched
interval allows.

**Four limits apply.** First, whether the pocket's constraint concerns IP₃ at
all is not settled: every residue within 15 Å of the ligand is also inside the
fold that holds it, and sequence cannot separate ligand binding from domain
packing. Mutational or binding-affinity data could. Second, a PI-PLC search
looks for one enzyme family. An absence says the canonical route to IP₃ is
missing, not that the receptor lacks a ligand, and the bounded null is equally
consistent with the site being held by something other than IP₃. Third, the
paired identities are measured against the human reference, so a module
difference is partly a difference in distance from human; the column-level
metrics, which are not referenced to one sequence, agree. Fourth, the pocket
is measured on human IP₃R3 and carried to the other two paralogues by
alignment, so a shell assignment in *ITPR1* is one transfer removed from a
structure. A PI-PLC whose catalytic halves have drifted past the profiles
would be scored absent; that moves a taxon into the test group and can only
weaken the null, not create it.
