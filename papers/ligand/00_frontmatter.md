## Abstract

The IP₃-binding core is the one part of the IP₃ receptor that the ryanodine
receptors, which share its fold and pore, do not share functionally. We asked
whether that core is under different constraint from the pore it gates.
Paired inside each orthologue, across 246–262 vertebrate orthologues per
paralogue, the pore module is more conserved than the ligand core in *ITPR1*
and *ITPR3* and level in *ITPR2*. All three paralogues reverse when the
luminal loop is counted as pore, so the comparison is only interpretable with
that boundary declared. Measured as distance to the ligand in six independent
IP₃-bound structures, the ten contact residues are more constrained than the
rest of the core and no more constrained than the rest of a pocket about
15 Å across, and per-site substitution rates show the same shape. Across 3,527
eukaryotic reference proteomes, 64 carry an IP₃ receptor and no
phosphoinositide-specific phospholipase C. Matched on divergence, their ligand
core is not relaxed, and the null is bounded by a ryanodine receptor control
that the matched test detects with essentially full power.
