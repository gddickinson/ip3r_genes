## Discussion

Every vertebrate genome in a declared scope of 309 that is able to hold an
IP₃ receptor gene on one contig carries all three paralogues, and no genome
in the scope, contiguous or not, reaches the state that would license a loss.
The count is zero on the family character and on each paralogue. That is an
unusual result for a gene family that has existed since the origin of the
vertebrates, since loss is the commonest fate of duplicated genes [R179, R193]
and a family with three members and five hundred million years of lineage
splitting has had many opportunities to shed one.

The zero is worth stating because the search that produced it has a measured
error rate, obtained from the same data. The false-negative rate, 140 of 923
cells overall, is not small, and a survey that reported zero losses without it
would be reporting mostly the quality of bird assemblies. The rate falls to 5
of 563 cells above the contiguity bar, and an independent series that shares
none of the state rules, the ryanodine receptor cell, falls to none of 189.
Absences in this data are concentrated exactly where assemblies are poor, and
where assemblies are good there are none. The contiguity bar that separates
the two was fixed from gene geometry before any error rate was measured and
survives calibration against both series; it is conservative against a
five per cent target and about right against one per cent.

Three consequences for how gene-loss surveys are done follow directly from
the measurements. First, an absence in a fragmented assembly is not evidence
of loss, and in this scope the fragmented assemblies are not a random subset:
the margin species, selected because their protein records looked
incomplete, are very largely the same genomes whose assemblies cannot hold
the gene. Selecting genomes for apparent absence and then searching them
selects for detection failure. Second, a paralogue-resolved absence is far
more fragile than a family-level one, manufactured in 18 of 32 analytical
settings against 2 of 32, because a fragment of one paralogue reads as
evidence against another when the paralogues are 61 to 68 % identical. Third,
the panel ablation says where a bait budget should go: paralogue coverage
buys recall and phylogenetic breadth within the vertebrates buys almost
none.

Retention of all three paralogues across the vertebrates is consistent with
each carrying a function the others cannot fully replace. The mouse and human
phenotypes of single-paralogue loss [R38, R45, R114] and the exocrine failure
of the double knockout [R115] point the same way. Dosage balance, which is
thought to retain duplicates from whole-genome duplications [R204], is
another explanation that this analysis cannot distinguish from
subfunctionalisation; a zero loss count is compatible with both.

**The result has limits that the data set.** The scope is 309 assemblies, and
nothing here extends to a lineage without one. Four cells in the two
cyclostomes are unassignable rather than present, because the bait panel
holds no labelled cyclostome *ITPR2* or *ITPR3*: the genomes carry three
family loci apiece, which is the expected count, but which locus is which
paralogue is not settled by this analysis. Paralogue labels throughout come
from the bait panel's labelled sequences, and the family-level count, which
needs no paralogue labels, is the more robust of the two. The one lesion
signal, an *ITPR3* excess concentrated in birds, sits mostly in assemblies
below the contiguity bar, where an indel excess cannot be separated from an
assembly effect; the lineage is named and the mechanism is not. And the
Markov rate models are refused rather than fitted, so the analysis states no
rate of loss, only that none is observed.

The strongest form of the claim is therefore the one that names its own
escape routes. A reader who wants a vertebrate IP₃ receptor loss from this
data can obtain one: by refusing all reconstruction evidence and ignoring
assembly contiguity together, which yields one genome at the family level,
or by resolving paralogues in fragmented assemblies, which yields up to 45
loss edges. Both are descriptions of an analytical setting, not of a genome.
