## Discussion

Purifying selection acts on the whole IP₃ receptor, and its strength is not
uniform along it. Two independent axes say where it is strongest. On the rate
axis, a whole-gene ω of 0.02 to 0.04 in every paralogue and a per-site screen
finding thousands of purifying sites and no positively selected one put the
receptor among strongly constrained proteins. On the conservation axis, the
gate and the selectivity filter are the most conserved elements on a
composition-free metric, and the gate is the one element of the channel that
all three paralogues have kept identical since the vertebrate genome
duplications ({paper:origin}). The weakest point lies about fifty residues
away, in a luminal loop of the same Pfam domain. The structural literature
places the filter and the gate at the heart of conduction and gating [R22,
R24, R59], and the conservation pattern agrees with it without having been
told where those elements are: the loop was located from the membrane
geometry, and the filter and gate by transferred structural coordinates with
anchor tests that could have failed.

The loop matters beyond its own score. A per-domain summary is the obvious
way to read a constraint map, and for this channel it gives the wrong answer:
with the loop left in, the pore domain of an ion channel reads as less
constrained than the linkers between its cytosolic domains. Any analysis
that summarises the receptor by Pfam domain will reproduce that artefact,
and the same boundary affects any comparison that uses the pore domain as a
reference. The loop is also where the maps of cryo-electron microscopy are
weakest, which is why we write unscored residues as missing rather than as
unconstrained on the painted structures.

The three copies have not been held to the same standard. ITPR1 is the most
constrained on one-ratio, two-ratio and relaxation tests alike, and its stem
is the only one whose branch-site rate is identifiable, with a class of about
a tenth of sites evolving faster than neutrally on the branch where the copy
was new. ITPR1 also carries almost all of the family's pathogenic missense
record [R38, R42, R43, R44]. We do not read the order of the other two as
settled. ITPR2 and ITPR3 differ in one-ratio ω by 0.0015, and ITPR2's near-empty
clinical record is better explained by ascertainment than by tolerance, since
its gate and IP₃ contacts are as conserved as ITPR1's.

For variant interpretation the practical result runs against the design of
the analysis. The deep within-paralogue orthologue sets, which took a
309-genome sweep to build, classify pathogenic against benign positions
better than the shallow control but worse than the family-wide and vertebrate
layers. Conservation across the family's full range, spanning paralogue
divergence and the non-vertebrate grade, is the more informative prior. A
resource for this family should therefore quote the family-wide layer first,
and population constraint [R210] would be a separate, complementary axis
rather than a replacement.

**Several limits bound these conclusions.** Synonymous sites are saturated
within every paralogue, so every ω is a tree-based estimate with a soft
denominator, and the two stems whose branch-site rate sits at the
optimiser's bound are unmeasured rather than large. The codon models
condition on the tree and its placement of the paralogue stems
({paper:origin}); a branch test cannot corroborate the topology it is run
on. The rate analyses are vertebrate-only, and nothing here bears on the
single-copy receptors outside the vertebrates ({paper:range}). The deep
layer is about 95 % genome translations; the curated-only recomputation
leaves the element ranking intact but cannot rule out every gene-model
error. The classifier is scored on 44 pathogenic and 34 benign positions,
most of them in ITPR1, so every AUC describes that set rather than the
family's variant space. Conservation and ω measure different things, a
column's dispersion and a substitution-rate ratio, and we report them side by
side without merging them. Finally, the stratification of uncertain variants
is not a classification, and should not be used as one.
