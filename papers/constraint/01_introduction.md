## Introduction

The inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor is a tetrameric calcium-release
channel of about 2,700 residues per subunit, gated by IP₃ and calcium at a
cytosolic ligand-binding region and conducting through a pore formed by the
last six transmembrane helices of each subunit [R02, R50, R24]. Cryo-electron
microscopy has located its working parts at residue resolution: the ten
residues within 4.5 Å of bound IP₃, the selectivity filter, and the
hydrophobic gate at the cytoplasmic end of the pore [R22, R24, R59].
Vertebrates carry three paralogues. Missense variants in *ITPR1* cause
spinocerebellar ataxias 15 and 29 and Gillespie syndrome [R38, R42, R43,
R44], loss of *ITPR2* function abolishes sweating [R45], and *ITPR3* variants
cause a demyelinating neuropathy and, in one recurrent case, a multisystem
disorder [R46, R47, R48].

Two kinds of evidence say how strongly a protein is held to its sequence, and
they are not the same measurement. A codon model estimates the ratio of
non-synonymous to synonymous substitution rates on a tree [R145]. It measures
selection directly, but it needs coding sequences that provably encode the
protein aligned, and it degrades where synonymous sites saturate. Per-residue
conservation measures how dispersed a column of an alignment is [R178, R171].
It needs no synonymous signal, but it needs many sequences per column, and a
column of a 2,700-residue protein cannot be scored from the dozen or so
orthologues a representative phylogenetic alignment holds per paralogue.
Population constraint from human variation [R210] answers a third question,
about the last few hundred thousand years, and is not what we measure here.

This paper asks where along the IP₃ receptor purifying selection acts. We
answer it on both axes and keep them apart. On the rate axis we fit
one-ratio, branch, branch-site and site models and a relaxation test to the
three vertebrate paralogues, and carry per-site rates onto the human
proteins. On the conservation axis we build orthologue sets deep enough to
score a residue, from one locus per genome per paralogue in a 309-genome
vertebrate sweep, and compare them against a shallow layer that shows what
the representative alignment alone would have given. We then place every
element of the receptor against the same protein's own linkers, test the
measured functional residues against their own elements, and ask whether the
result classifies the family's clinical missense variants.

Three things make the answer less obvious than it looks. The Pfam signature
named after the ligand, PF08709, contains none of the ten measured IP₃
contacts, which sit in MIR and RIH, the domains the family shares with the
ryanodine receptors [R05, R57, R58]. The pore domain contains a stretch that
no annotation carries and that behaves unlike the rest of it. And the
clinical record is mostly uncertain, so a classifier trained on it is scored
on very few positions. Each is reported with the control that measures it.
