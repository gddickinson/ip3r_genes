## Abstract

The inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor (IP₃R) is the calcium-release
channel of the endoplasmic reticulum, and vertebrates carry three paralogues,
ITPR1, ITPR2 and ITPR3, whose missense variants cause ataxias, anhidrosis and
neuropathy. Where along its 2,700 residues purifying selection acts has not
been measured on alignments deep enough to score a residue. We fitted codon
models to 57 vertebrate coding sequences, each proved to encode the protein
it was aligned as, and computed per-residue conservation on four nested
alignment layers, the deepest holding 249 to 265 genome-derived orthologues of
each human paralogue. All three paralogues are under strong purifying
selection (one-ratio ω 0.0238 to 0.0430), and ITPR1 is the most constrained
and is intensified relative to its sisters (RELAX k = 9.36). Along the
protein, the gate and the selectivity filter are the most conserved elements
in all three paralogues on a composition-free metric, and the gate is
identical between all three human copies. A 50-residue luminal loop inside the pore domain, located from
the structure's membrane geometry rather than from any annotation, is the
least conserved sequence in the receptor; left inside the pore domain it makes
the pore look less constrained than the linkers. Scored as a classifier of
1,753 ClinVar missense records, conservation separates pathogenic from benign
positions best on the family-wide layer (AUC 0.872), and the deep
within-paralogue layer is third of four. Because 1,546 records (88 %) are of
uncertain significance, we release every residue's scores as a
stratification resource and do not use it to call variants.
