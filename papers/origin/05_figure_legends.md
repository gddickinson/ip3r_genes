## Figure legends

**{fig:tree}.** A maximum-likelihood tree of 134 representatives places the
three vertebrate paralogues in three clades, rooted on the six ryanodine
receptors in the purple box; every other protein is an IP₃ receptor. Each
coloured box is one paralogue's whole clade, which holds 19 proteins for
*ITPR1*, 13 for *ITPR2* and 19 for *ITPR3*; each clade is supported at 100/100
(SH-aLRT/UFBoot, printed beside the box). Filled coloured dots are proteins whose database record names
the paralogue. Open circles are seven shark, chimaera and coelacanth proteins
whose records name none; the tree places four in *ITPR1*, two in *ITPR2* and
one in *ITPR3*. Counted on names alone, the largest *ITPR1* clade holds 13
proteins at 47.8/95, because those unnamed genes sit interleaved at its base.
The six grey vertebrate proteins outside the boxes are hagfish and lamprey
genes whose paralogue cannot be assigned: four form their own clade (99.5/100)
branching first among the 57 vertebrate proteins, and two join the *ITPR2* +
*ITPR3* clade at 83.1/77. Grey proteins above the vertebrates are invertebrate,
protist, plant and fungal receptors, bracketed and counted on the right.
Backbone numbers give the support of the nodes joining the clades, and the two
weak values mark where the jawless-fish proteins attach. A filled black dot
marks every node that clears both SH-aLRT ≥ 80 and UFBoot ≥ 95, and no tip is
ringed, because the relabelling rule moved no tip.

**{fig:sister}.** Two of the three possible sister pairings fall outside the
95 % confidence set of topologies. (a) Each constrained topology is plotted by
the log-likelihood it costs against the unconstrained tree. (b) The
approximately unbiased p-value over 10,000 RELL replicates is plotted on the
same row of categories, with the rejection line at p = 0.05. Two panels are
used rather than twin axes because the two measures have unrelated scales. The
pairing of *ITPR2* with *ITPR3* is the only one the test retains.

**{fig:neighbourhood}.** Both flanking-gene families that survive between the
three human neighbourhoods touch *ITPR1*. (a) The *ITPR1*, *ITPR2* and *ITPR3*
neighbourhoods are drawn as gene tracks, with families shared between two loci
shaded and linked. (b) Each shared family's prevalence across the swept genomes
is set against the rate at which random windows carry it. The tracks are read
from a committed table, so the figure cannot show a gene the data lack, and no
shading connects the *ITPR2* and *ITPR3* tracks.

**{fig:paralogon}.** The *ITPR1* neighbourhood is paralogous to both others
across the vertebrates, while *ITPR2* against *ITPR3* sits at the background.
(a) Bars give the fraction of genomes in which one neighbourhood carries a
paralogue of the other, with the matched random-window rate drawn across them.
(b) The single human genome is tested against a real-window permutation null,
pair by pair, with the three ryanodine receptor pairs beside the IP₃ receptor
pairs. The sister family behaves the same way through the same instrument,
which is what makes the weak human result a property of the test.

**{fig:dated}.** The two duplications map to two different branches of the
dated vertebrate tree. Nodes are placed on a linear time axis, and the spread
of published ages is drawn as a band on the nodes a placement uses. Markers
show each distinct placement once, with its tip treatment, since repeating the
same placement for every matrix cell would make agreement look like weight.
The older bracket is left open because nothing in the sampled tree closes it.

**{fig:teleost}.** The two teleost *ITPR1* copies divide one ancestral block
between them. (a) Each two-copy genome is placed by how many ancestral-block
symbols each copy retains, so a point away from both axes is a genome in which
both copies keep part of the block. (b) Bars count the genomes meeting that
condition and the stricter one that the two symbol sets are disjoint, against
a tetrapod and a pre-duplication ray-finned reference, beside a line at all 49
genomes. Disjoint sets are what reciprocal loss after one duplication leaves.

**{fig:introns}.** The three paralogues share their intron positions in every
genome tested, and the ryanodine receptors do not. (a) Intron positions within
each paralogue are ranked by the fraction of genomes that carry them, with the
90 % line drawn. (b) Shared positions are plotted against the number expected
by chance, one point per genome and pair, with the identity line. Every IP₃
receptor pair sits far above the line, and every pairing with the ryanodine
receptors sits on it.
