## Abstract

Vertebrates carry three inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor genes,
*ITPR1*, *ITPR2* and *ITPR3*. Which two are sisters, when the duplications
that made them happened, and whether they are products of the early
vertebrate genome duplications have not been measured against a declared
sample. We answered each question with a different instrument, and each
instrument was run beside a control that could have failed. A
maximum-likelihood tree of 134 representative proteins, rooted on the
ryanodine receptors, makes *ITPR2* and *ITPR3* sisters. The approximately
unbiased test rejects both alternative pairings (p < 2 × 10⁻⁵) and does not
reject this one (p = 0.476). Reconciliation against a literature-calibrated
species tree places the split of *ITPR1* from the other two on the vertebrate
stem and the split of *ITPR2* from *ITPR3* on the gnathostome stem, bracketed
462–563 Ma, and the placement holds across five topologies and all 112
rootings. The genomic neighbourhoods are paralogous: across 309 genomes the
*ITPR1* neighbourhood carries a paralogue of the *ITPR2* neighbourhood in 141
of 175 genomes and of the *ITPR3* neighbourhood in 89 of 152, against 2.6 %
of matched random windows. *ITPR2* against *ITPR3* sits at that background,
which does not corroborate the tree's sister pair. The teleost duplication
doubled *ITPR1* alone, into two copies that partition one ancestral block.
The three paralogues share a median 46 to 49 intron positions where 0.55 are
expected, in every genome tested. The ryanodine receptors, which carry every
Pfam domain the family is diagnosed by, share a median of one.
