## Extended Data

**{fig:alignment}.** Trimming leaves an alignment that almost every
representative covers. (**a**) Per-column conservation of the trimmed
alignment is drawn under human *ITPR1*'s domain architecture, which is mapped
through the alignment by walking the human row rather than by scaling residue
coordinates onto columns. (**b**) Each representative's coverage of the
trimmed alignment is plotted by group; the median is 0.96 and one tip of 134
covers less than half.

**{fig:identity}.** All-pairs identity over mutually covered columns separates
the vertebrate paralogue groups from each other and from the outgroup. The
matrix is ordered and side-barred by group, and the three vertebrate paralogue
blocks stand out from a dark cross-family field. Gaps are not counted as
mismatches, so a partial sequence does not look divergent.

**{fig:group_identity}.** The three paralogues sit at 0.74 to 0.79 identity to
one another and 0.91 within themselves. The left panel ranks the three
paralogue pairs and the right panel puts every group pair on one scale. By
identity alone *ITPR1* and *ITPR2* are the closest pair, which the tree
contradicts.

**{fig:support}.** Most nodes clear both support tests, and the tree disputes
no census name. (**a**) Every internal node is plotted by SH-aLRT against
ultrafast bootstrap, with the joint threshold region shaded and the claim
nodes ringed. (**b**) The vertebrate tips the tree places in no paralogue
clade are listed with the support of the clade each lands in; reciprocal best
hits uphold the database name for all five disputed names.

**{fig:synteny}.** Neighbourhood similarity confirms the paralogue cells and
finds nothing across the family boundary. (**a**) Mean flanking-gene
similarity is shown for each pair class beside its matched random-window
control, one locus per species. (**b**) The paralogue caller's scores on
annotation-confirmed loci and on random windows are shown with the threshold
sweep, where the operating point is set at the largest gap between call rate
and false-call rate. (**c**) Within-paralogue similarity is split into pairs
inside one vertebrate class and pairs across classes, where *ITPR3*'s
neighbourhood falls well below its two siblings.

**{fig:reconciliation}.** The dating holds across topologies, rootings and
tip treatments, and a loss count read off it would count sampling. (**a**)
Each cell of the five-topology by three-treatment matrix names its deepest
paralogue duplication, and every rooting of the vertebrate subtree is scored
by total events with the outgroup and minimum-event rootings marked. (**b**)
Root-to-tip distances of all 57 vertebrate tips are drawn with the six
cyclostome loci marked, beside the neighbourhood call for each locus. (**c**)
The implied losses are classified against the genome sweep, and none is
corroborated in any cell of the matrix.

**{fig:duplication}.** Copy number follows each lineage's duplication history,
and the teleost copies come from one event. (**a**) Mean copies per genome of
each paralogue and of the ryanodine receptors are grouped by whole-genome
duplication status, beside the fraction of genomes carrying more than one
copy by vertebrate class. (**b**) Each anchor genome's cross-anchor
assignment margin is positive, and the multi-copy counts stay flat across
seven coverage bars.

**{fig:architecture}.** The exon count is conserved while the genomic span is
not, and the instrument that measures both is checked against the genome.
(**a**) Coding exons, genomic span on a logarithmic axis, and the median exon
count across six coverage bars are drawn for each paralogue and the ryanodine
control. (**b**) The unspliceable fraction of junctions is drawn on the
complement, by gap bin, and beside agreement with independently annotated
coding edges. (**c**) Every split and fragmentary annotated model receives a
verdict, its internal termini are placed against the exon structure, and the
duplication detector is scored against the committed copy count.

**{fig:representative_alignment}.** Trimming kept the dense columns and cut
the sparse ones. The input alignment's occupancy is rasterised in its own
coordinates for all 134 representatives, its mean occupancy is plotted along
the same axis, and the kept columns are ticked beneath. Only one raster is
drawn because the trimmed alignment shares no x-axis with its input.
