## Discussion

Three instruments that share almost no evidence give a consistent account of
where the three vertebrate IP₃ receptors came from. The genomic
neighbourhoods are paralogous, with a vertebrate-dated ohnologue pair that
replicates across the vertebrates against a real-window null, so at least one
of the duplications copied a block of genome and not a single gene. The dated
reconciliation puts the two duplications on two different branches: the
first, which separated *ITPR1* from the ancestor of *ITPR2* and *ITPR3*, on
the vertebrate stem, and the second on the gnathostome stem, bracketed
462–563 Ma. Both positions are where the two rounds of early vertebrate
duplication are usually placed [R180, R182]. The intron positions add a
character that no protein alignment can produce: the three paralogues carry
one inherited exon structure, conserved to within a few introns across 189
genomes, and the ryanodine receptors carry an unrelated one.

**One disagreement stays open, and we report it rather than resolve it.** The
tree makes *ITPR2* and *ITPR3* sisters and rejects both alternatives. The
neighbourhood makes *ITPR1* the block that kept a flanking ohnologue with
each of the other two, and *ITPR2* with *ITPR3* the one pair that retains
nothing above background. These are different quantities. The order of
branching is a statement about when copies diverged, while a retained flank
records which copies of each neighbour survived hundreds of millions of years
of reciprocal deletion, a process that reconstructions of the vertebrate
ancestral genome show to be lineage-specific and uncoupled from duplication
order [R183, R205]. The reconciliation is consistent with both readings,
because it makes *ITPR1* the earlier-diverging copy, the copy that could keep
a shared flank with each of the others. That is suggestive rather than a
test. Preferring whichever instrument agrees with a headline is how an
analysis of this size talks itself into a wrong answer.

**The dating rests on six tips, and we say so.** The vertebrate-stem
placement is forced by one cyclostome clade that sits among the gnathostome
paralogues. We measured the two standard objections, weak support and long
branches, and neither applies: collapsing every unsupported node keeps the
placement, and the cyclostome tips sit at the median root-to-tip distance.
The placement is still decided by where one two-tip clade attaches, and a
denser cyclostome sample is the experiment that would test it.

**The teleost result is clean and its cause is not in this evidence.** The
third duplication copied all three paralogues, as the doubled ryanodine
receptors in the same genomes show, and *ITPR1* alone kept its second copy in
nearly every teleost. The two copies divide one ancestral block and were made
by one event. Why *ITPR1* alone was retained is a question for expression and
function, which this paper does not measure.

**The limits are the instruments' own.** Four stand out. First, which of the
two early rounds made which split cannot be separated here: the dated link is
dated to Vertebrata, which covers both rounds, and separating them needs the
cyclostome side of the quartet, where the neighbourhood caller could not
reach, because a genome in which fewer than a third of the flanking genes
carry a name gives the caller nothing to compare. Second, the genome-wide
block scan finds no block that looks like the missing fourth member of the
quartet. With one dated ohnologue pair surviving between the blocks that do
carry a gene, a block that lost the gene too has nothing left to be
recognised by, so this is a limit of the evidence and not a claim that the
fourth slot never existed. Third, the single human genome is underpowered for
the two-round test for both families alike, which is why the replication
across genomes carries the claim. Fourth, the AU test's confidence set holds
two trees, and both carry the same sister pair; a different alignment could
move deeper nodes but would have to move the pair out of a set that has
already excluded both alternatives.

**This paper makes no claim about losses or function.** It does not count losses: the
reconciliation's implied losses are sampling or cannot be assessed, none is
corroborated, and a loss count needs the genomes themselves. It does not say what the paralogues do
differently. It answers where they came from, and the answer is that the
three IP₃ receptors of vertebrates are ohnologues from two duplications on
two different branches of the early vertebrate tree, carrying one ancestral
exon structure that their sister family does not share.
