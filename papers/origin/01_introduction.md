## Introduction

Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate releases calcium from the endoplasmic
reticulum [R01] through a tetrameric channel of about 2,700 residues per
subunit [R02, R03]. From its first sequence the channel was recognised as the
smaller relative of the ryanodine receptor [R04], and the two families share
the MIR, RIH and RIH-associated domains and the six-transmembrane pore. In
vertebrates the IP₃ receptor is encoded by three genes, *ITPR1*, *ITPR2* and
*ITPR3* [R32], which differ in tissue distribution [R29, R33], in
single-channel behaviour [R67] and in the diseases their variants cause
[R38, R45, R46].

How one ancestral gene became three is usually answered by assumption. The
vertebrate lineage went through two rounds of whole-genome duplication
[R179, R180], and the teleost fishes through a third [R181, R184], so a
three-member vertebrate family with a single invertebrate orthologue reads
naturally as the product of those events. Three questions are hidden in that
reading, and none of them has been measured for this family. The first is
the branching order: which two of the three paralogues are sisters, which no
published, support-annotated maximum-likelihood analysis rooted on the
ryanodine receptors has fixed. The second is the timing: where on the
vertebrate tree each duplication falls, which a gene tree cannot say on its
own. The third is the mechanism: whether the duplications copied the
surrounding genome as well as the gene, which is the evidence that separates
a whole-genome duplication from a local one.

Each question needs a different kind of evidence, and each kind can mislead
in a way the others cannot detect. A sequence tree can be pulled by long
branches, by model violation and by mislabelled tips. A reconciliation
inherits every error of the gene tree and of the species tree it is mapped
onto. A neighbourhood comparison can be driven by annotation depth or by
naming conventions rather than by the genome. We therefore used three
instruments that share as little as possible: a tree, a dated reconciliation,
and the genomic neighbourhood read in two independent ways. A fourth
character, the positions of the introns, is one no protein alignment
produces. Every one of them was run beside a control that could have failed.

The control that matters most is built into the family. The ryanodine
receptors carry every Pfam domain used to diagnose the IP₃ receptors, which
makes them the hardest decoy for any search, as the companion paper on the
family's range shows ({paper:range}). Here the same property makes them the
most useful control. They root the tree, they are a second three-member
vertebrate family whose origin in the early duplications is not in doubt, and
they pass through every instrument in the same genomes as the IP₃ receptors.
Where an instrument reads the ryanodine trio correctly, its reading of the
IP₃ trio can be taken at face value. Where it cannot, the limit belongs to
the instrument.

The scope is declared and finite. The tree is built on 134 representative
proteins chosen by stated rules from a census of 18,065 records. The
neighbourhoods are read at every IP₃ and ryanodine receptor locus recovered
from 309 vertebrate genome assemblies, and the gene architecture in the 189 of
those assemblies contiguous enough to carry the whole gene. The species tree
is an input taken from the literature and is not estimated here.
