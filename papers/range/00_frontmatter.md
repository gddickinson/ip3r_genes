# The IP₃ receptor is ancestrally eukaryotic and has been lost repeatedly outside the animals

## Abstract

The inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor (IP₃R) is the endoplasmic
reticulum's ligand-gated calcium-release channel. Its distribution beyond the
animals has been inferred from small samples, and every search for it is
exposed to the ryanodine receptors (RyRs), which carry all of its diagnostic
Pfam domains. We asked in which eukaryotic lineages an IP₃ receptor gene
exists, and answered with instruments that call each record positively as one
family or the other and that were benchmarked on 31 decoys before use. Across
763 vertebrate and 6,928 other reference proteomes, the census holds 18,065
records, 8,990 of them IP₃ receptors across 1,402 taxa. The family is present
in 662 of the 6,928 non-vertebrate proteomes, across animals, several protist
lineages, green algae and early-diverging fungi, and in none of 634 archaeal
or 3,537 bacterial proteomes. It is absent from all 384 land-plant and all
1,353 Dikarya proteomes while present in 15 of 48 chlorophyte and 18 of 34
mucoromycote ones. We took 35 clade-level absences to 194 genome assemblies,
in which a positive control chosen per clade by measurement demonstrated
that the search could recover a long, conserved, multi-exon gene. All 35
absences hold at assembly level. All 99 plant and fungal records were chased
individually: 47 are real genes, 52 are fragments, and none is contamination.
The family is therefore ancestral to eukaryotes and has been lost
independently in land plants, in Dikarya and in several other lineages. Copy
number outside the vertebrates runs from zero to eighteen, and the shape of
the few structures available separates the two families as the sequence
census does.
